Example: Visualizing Mean Trends with Uncertainty

mass_compare_plot <- penguins |>
  group_by(species, island) |>
  summarise(
    mean_mass = mean(body_mass_g, na.rm = TRUE),
    sd_mass = sd(body_mass_g, na.rm = TRUE),
    n = n(),
    .groups = "drop"
  ) |>
  ggplot(aes(x = species, y = mean_mass, colour = island)) +
  geom_point(size = 3) +
  geom_errorbar(aes(ymin = mean_mass - sd_mass,
                    ymax = mean_mass + sd_mass), 
                width = 0.2) +
  labs(title = "Mean Body Mass by Species and Island",
       subtitle = "Error bars represent standard deviation", 
       y = "Mean Body Mass",
       x = "Species") +
  theme_minimal()

mass_compare_plot

The species Gentoo have the highest mean body mass compared to the Adelle and Chinstrap penguins. Adelle and Chinstrap penguins displayed a small difference in their mean body mass, suggesting that the difference may not be statically significant. However, a statistical test would be required for confirmation.

The Adelle species are observed on all three islands, with small difference in body mass, ranging from 3600 g to 3800 g. Chinstrap are observed on Dream and display a similar mean body mass to Adelle penguins. However, the Gentoo penguins observed on Biscoe island, have a considerably higher mean body mass than the other two species. This suggests that a statistical test may reveal significant differences in mean body mass between Gentoo penguins and the other species.

From an ecological perspective, Biscoe Island may provide favourable environmental conditions for Gentoo penguins. Alternatively, Gentoo penguins may naturally have a larger body size due to their biological characteristics.
